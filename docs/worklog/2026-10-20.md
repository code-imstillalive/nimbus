## Findings

- **A Home Assistant backup blinds the solar running-low gate.** HA's backup
  locks the recorder database for writes (`recorder/backup.py`,
  `async_pre_backup`). A manual backup started at 07:17, and the gate's 30-minute
  PV mean froze on the last row written before the lock: 1.297 kW against a live
  7.8 kW at 08:28, ratio 0.29. The rest of the day was planned on 30 % of the
  forecast, the battery held at its reserve and spare solar was exported. When
  the lock released at about 08:46, the gate read 6.1 kW (ratio 1.13), turned
  off, and the plan charged from solar. Fixed in #1916.
- **The direction-change margin held the battery idle for under a cent.** Period
  0 was one minute long, so holding idle while 6.5 kW was exported cost $0.0002
  over the horizon, and the 1c margin kept it. #1917's floor stops the margin
  keeping a cheaper-selling plan.
- **The reserve only protected a window already running.** At a window opening
  later in the horizon, `held` could start at 0 for free, so a plan solved in the
  morning sold the battery to 2 % at 03:00 at 27.5c against a 30c release price.
  A synthetic replay of the same night held 12.0 kWh when solved at 00:00 and sold
  to 2.4 kWh when solved at 22:00. In practice the plans that dispatch after
  midnight are solved inside the window, where the reserve bound. Fixed in #1918.
- **Solar beyond the plan's forecast is exported.** With the battery commanded to
  the plan's charge rate, solar above the plan's (risk-adjusted) forecast has
  nowhere to go but the grid. #1917 does not cover this: the plan itself
  exported nothing. It needs a rule where the plan becomes a battery command.
