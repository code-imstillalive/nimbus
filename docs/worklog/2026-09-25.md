### The move that cracked it: an instrument from a different pipeline

The `feedback_nimbus_stop_daily_release_churn` lesson — *check the record first, cross-check against an independent instrument* — is what worked, and it worked in one step. `sensor.lv_v2_p2p_confirmed_history` carries the real settled LocalVolts revenue, produced by a pipeline with no connection to the scorer:

| day | settled P2P export | volume | EPR published |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 Sep | **$4.01** | 21.9 kWh | **88.6%** |
| 21 Sep | $14.91 | 49.4 kWh | 51.9% |
| 22 Sep | $12.71 | 57.9 kWh | 38.5% |
| 23 Sep | $13.46 | 50.0 kWh | 36.4% |

**Anti-correlated with the money.** The scorer read worst on the household's best-earning days and read 88.6% on the one day it genuinely under-exported by half. That is not a calibration problem, it is a sign error in what the metric is tracking — and it settled "is this the score or the dispatch?" before a line of the scorer was opened.

It also has a consequence worth stating plainly: **EPR on this install could not have detected a real dispatch failure.** #1179 records 59% of solve cycles failing for 2.7 h inside the P2P window on the night of 20 Sep — the day that scored best.

### Root cause

`quality_report.py:749` — `j_ach = j_ach_residual.total_cost - real_p2p_dollars_earned`.

A day is first scored just after local midnight. Its settlement does not arrive until **~09:00 local the following morning** (confirmed from the settlement sensor's own `last_updated`: 2026-09-23T23:00Z = 24 Sep 09:00 AEST, carrying `latest_date: 2026-09-23`). Until it does, `real_p2p_dollars` is 0 — and that *also* makes the `real_p2p_volume_kwh > 0.01` bonus gate false, so the bonus-priced `grid_oracle` rebuild never runs either. `j_ref`, `j_ach` and `j_star` go P2P-blind **together**, and the day is priced as though the household had no P2P arrangement at all.

#1082 already existed for exactly this, and was genuinely on production (shipped v0.94.380, well before 0.94.413). Its repair is reachable only from inside the `latest_date == yesterday_key` fast path, **so it expires at local midnight.** The production log:

```
2026-09-24 01:00  2026-09-23 was scored before its settlement was available
2026-09-24 02:00-06:00  same, hourly
2026-09-24 06:00  LAST firing
2026-09-24 09:00  settlement for 23 Sep arrives ($13.46)   <- nothing rescores it
2026-09-25 00:00  yesterday_key rolls over; the row is frozen at 36.4%
```

Identical shape on 22 Sep. **Why it stopped at 06:00 is not established.** The 06:00 ML retrain does starve the solve cycle (`previous cycle still in progress -- skipping this tick`, up to 5 consecutive skips) but that burst ends at 06:07, which does not explain silence until midnight. Left on #1200 as an open question rather than given a tidy cause, because this issue's history is tidy causes that did not survive.

### What shipped — v0.94.416

- **`"p"` on a history row** whose figures were computed without settlement. Written only when provisional, self-clearing on re-score, one character. The attribute payload was already measured 27% over #944's 16 KB cap, so correct rows must cost nothing — hence the opposite convention to `"r"`, which is always written. Safe here because the sweep reads an absent key as "nothing to do": a wrongly-absent key costs a missed repair, never a wrong one.
- **`repair_provisional_quality_history()`** — re-scores any *past* flagged row once the real settlement entry is present. **No deadline.** Gated on `_settlement_entry_exists()` so a day whose settlement never arrives costs **zero** solves rather than one MILP per cycle, which would be #773/#757's executor starvation rebuilt on purpose. One day per cycle, oldest first. Checks *presence*, not a non-zero figure, because a settled day of genuinely zero export is a real result.
- **A conditional "Re-score with settlement" button on the Regret card.** Household ask: *"can we actually just add this rescore fix script in a form of a clickable button on the forecaster view next to epr table?"* Built into the card rather than as a dashboard button — it then needs **no lovelace edit at all**, which matters because that dashboard is under manual redesign and carries card-mod highlights that have been lost five times. Shown only when the displayed day is actually waiting; an always-present button invites buying oracle solves on a day that is already correct.

### The chain, in the order it actually came apart

**#1086 said the configured 85.8% round-trip was "~6 points pessimistic".** Three closed-loop days implied one-way `e` of 0.978-0.986. The method is sound algebra and genuinely insensitive to SoC error (+/-2 kWh of `stored_delta` moves `e` by +/-0.009).

**It fails a sanity check nobody applied.** Those figures imply round-trips of **0.972 and 0.964**. A 96-97% round-trip battery does not exist; real LFP AC-to-AC is 85-92%. That should have been read as evidence against the measurement.

**An appealing wrong answer, killed by arithmetic.** The charge and discharge legs sit at 0.848 and 1.152, and `eta**2` at the configured 0.9263 is 0.858 and 1.166 -- a match within ~1% **on two legs of opposite sign**, which is not something noise produces. Recomputing the whole trajectory with the `eta` term removed moved the mean gap 9.88 -> 9.07. Not the cause. A five-line script was the only thing between that and a confidently wrong issue.

**The real answer was already in the record.** `docs/handover/2026-09-17-epr-and-scoring.md`, band-matched over SoC 67-83.5%, at the configured `e`: **discharge implies 123.8 kWh** (within 1.3% of the settled 122.2) while **charge implies 101.3 kWh** (17% low). The discharge leg independently validates 0.9263 without touching the charge measurement at all. The same section records a band-matched round-trip of **1.049** -- above 100%.

So **85.8% stands**, and the household confirmed: not changing it. #1086 closed with the reasoning, not just the decision.

**But that doc declared the opposite as RESOLVED.** Its heading read *"RESOLVED: the counters are sound, the SoC scale is DIRECTION-DEPENDENT / Charge does not under-count"*. The inference does not hold: a closed SoC loop cancels a direction-dependent **SoC-scale** error, not an **energy-measurement** error on the charge leg. It substitutes one for the other, and #1086 inherited the conflation. Withdrawn in place rather than deleted, because handover docs get read as settled fact.

**#1231 filed for the actual defect**: the charge-energy measurement reads 12-21% low and the error grows with power. Three independent lines now agree -- the 17 Sep capacity cross-check (17% low), the 24 Sep hourly reconstruction (+3.55/+4.66/+5.25 pt unaccounted at 30.2/32.3/18.7 kW), and two impossible round-trips. Explicitly *not* a live-dispatch defect: the LP is priced from the configured value, which the clean discharge leg supports.

**Asked whether fixing this would move EPR. Verified by call graph rather than assumed:** `quality_report.py` never imports `backtest.py`, and `score_candidate_day`/`run_efficiency_sensitivity_sweep` are called only from `backtest.py` and `compute_efficiency_backtest_report`. So the backtest work cannot touch EPR. But `quality_report.py:437` builds j_ach's trajectory from `actual_charge_kw * charge_efficiency * hours` -- so **#1231 does corrupt EPR**, and the direction and size have deliberately not been guessed.

### #1232 -- the backtest sensor was wrong twice

Found by reading the live entity while checking something else. `sensor.nimbus_efficiency_backtest` had two independent defects, both on every install.

**Circular scoring.** `score_candidate_day()` planned with the candidate battery and scored with that same battery -- its own docstring said so. A candidate assuming 99% efficiency planned more cycling, was charged 1% loss per leg, and reached a higher final SoC credited at `salvage_value`. Measured live: 85% -> -33.32, 90% -> -34.99, 95% -> -36.53, 99% -> -37.74. **Strictly monotonic, and necessarily so**, which made `best_candidate` always the largest candidate. Published as `best_candidate: 99%` next to a configured 85.8 -- reading as advice toward a setting `BatteryConfig` rejects at 100% and the wizard warns against.

**The fix validates itself.** Plan with the candidate, score under the configured battery, recomputing the SoC trajectory under the scoring battery's own efficiencies instead of inheriting the candidate's. On the test scenario (real round-trip 90.25%) the sweep now picks **90.25%** -- the truth -- and is no longer monotonic. A sweep that identifies the physics it runs on is measuring something.

The configured value is now swept too; it never was, so "is my setting best" was unanswerable on any install. Plus `undeliverable_kwh` per candidate, for plans the real pack could not have delivered.

**Dollars published as a percentage.** The state is `spread_dollars`; the integration declared `_attr_native_unit_of_measurement = "%"` with the comment *"Configured efficiency is a percentage"* -- which is exactly the misreading. Live: `4.4211 %` meaning $4.42, and with `state_class: MEASUREMENT`, HA recording long-term statistics in percent for a currency value. The standalone writer always posted `"$"`, so **the two deployment paths disagreed about one sensor's unit** and every HACS install got the wrong one. Fixed to `hass.config.currency` -- not `"$"`, which would be wrong for any non-dollar household.

**Two tests had encoded the bug as an invariant.** `test_results_are_labeled_and_monotonic_with_efficiency` asserted the sweep *is* monotonic and called that *"the real, interpretable 'does efficiency actually matter here' signal this whole feature exists to produce."* Replaced by its opposite, history preserved in the new docstring. ### devhub, measured rather than assumed

**The mistake worth recording.** I read `sensor.nimbus_efficiency_backtest` on devhub, got values identical to production, and called it an independent reproduction on a second install. It was **production's mirror** -- `nimbus_version: 0.94.417`, `unique_id` prefixed `3d37242f4b494bfc_`. This project's own CLAUDE.md records sixteen occurrences of exactly this, and **my own worklog from 21 Sep already said `..._2` is devhub's own entity.** Third time in one day of re-deriving something already written down (after the battery sign convention and the handover's RESOLVED claim). **Then the mechanism, from source rather than inference.** `remote_homeassistant/__init__.py:729`: `unique_id = f"{entry.unique_id[:16]}_{entity_id}"`, where `entry.unique_id` is the remote instance's UUID.

**A second appealing hypothesis, also killed.** 116KAT's CLAUDE.md documents that HA's duplicate-instance repair regenerates the instance ID -- which looked like the unexplained trigger. **What the evidence actually supports** is that the Live Open Item conflates two phenomena:
- **Mass orphaning (~7,000 / ~3,000) is prefix changes**, not spontaneous. Changing `entity_prefix` rewrites every unique_id at once. `sensor.mirror_*` is debris from the 2026-09-01 incident when the prefix was `mirror_`. **Counts, first time measured:** 9,972 entities total, **3,190 dead `mirror_*`** (every one unavailable), 3,233 ending `_2` of which only **64 are live and 22 of those are Nimbus** (devhub's own -- explicitly out of scope for deletion), 6,802 unavailable overall (**68% of the instance**).

Mirrored entities move to `sensor.home_*`, devhub's Nimbus reclaims the canonical ids, collisions end permanently. Cost is one final bounded orphaning, then one cleanup pass.

**And a genuine blocker worth knowing.** `ha_remove_entity` works on these orphans -- 4 of 4 deleted by hand. That explains the historical note that "Claude's own bulk-removal attempts were blocked", which had been read as an HA limitation.

**Cost note that changed the plan:** each devhub restart triggers a reconnect, which spawns a fresh orphan set. Devhub validation is not free, so #1232 ships unreleased until the mirror is sorted -- per the household's rule that nothing releases as a fix before a thorough test.
