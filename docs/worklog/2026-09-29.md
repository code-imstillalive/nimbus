## #1406 — the proximal anchor is a switching cost (merged)

`solver/network.py`'s `_add_proximal_penalty` was `penalty_cost =
proximal_weight * hours[new_idx]`. It is now `penalty_cost = proximal_weight`,
the parameter dropped from the signature and all three call sites, and the module
docstring's `$/kWh-equivalent` corrected to `$ per kW of deviation`.

**Why it mattered on the reference household.** The published plan is tiered:
1-minute periods near-term, then 5-minute, then 30-minute. Scaling a tie-break by
period duration therefore made it **weakest on the shortest periods — which are
period[0] and [1], the only two that ever become a real inverter command.** At the
live weight a full 40 kW reversal on a 1-minute period cost **$0.0067**, and the
combined deterrent on the dispatch head was about $0.037. Measured over one
3-hour charge window: `period[0]` snapped to exactly `0.000` on **18 of 400
samples** and back to the -40 kW clamp, `+0.000 -> -40.000` at 11:15:21 and back
sixteen seconds later, producing **26 inverter command writes in three hours**
while the inverter's own mode never changed once.

**Every one of those zeros coincided to the second with a healthy solve**
(1.13–1.74 s), which is what rules out a failed solve or a stale write: the LP
genuinely answered "charge 0 kW now" and, seconds later, "charge 40 kW now".
Classic degeneracy — and the penalty built to break exactly that tie was scaled so
it was weakest where dispatch reads.

### I filed this claiming both penalties were wrong. Half of it was wrong.

The issue, and 116KAT's own root-cause entry, both said *"make both stability
penalties switching costs — drop the `hours[t]` factor."* **The intra-plan
smoothness penalty must keep it.** What smoothness trades against is energy
arbitrage, whose gain is `spread × kW × hours`, so `hours` sits on **both sides**
of the comparison and cancels — the suppression threshold is already uniform
across tiers, which is the property the argument for changing it was trying to
achieve. The proximal anchor has no such cancelling term, because it trades
against "walk away from what the hardware is already doing", which costs the same
whatever the slot length.

Caught before implementing, corrected and retitled the issue, and
`tests/test_1406_proximal_anchor_is_a_switching_cost.py` pins **both** halves so
the asymmetry cannot be tidied away later. 116KAT PR #867 corrects that repo's own
prescription for the same reason.

**Effect, on the golden master rather than in argument:** of 17 scenarios only the
two `_two_cycles` ones move at all; `cycles[0]` is byte-identical in both (a first
solve has no previous plan to anchor to), and in `cycles[1]` the median change is
**0.0005 kW**, the largest **0.357 kW**.

**A promise I should not have made.** The PR body and a commit message both said I
would validate this by measuring `period[0]` on devhub. **#1396's own body says
devhub cannot do that** — its solve-output entities are squatted by NUC1's
mirrored rows, so devhub's sensors read *production's* data. I had measured that
mirror myself the same morning. Reading #1396 in full is what caught it, after the
claim was already written down twice.

## Phase 4 (#1303) — plan assembly out of `main()`

The 244-line span that built every `elements.*Config` and called
`network.build_plan()` is now `solver_plan.py`'s `assemble_and_solve_plan()`.

**This is the first phase that cannot be proved.** Phases 1–3 relocated existing
named functions, so the bytes could be diffed and the ASTs compared. Here the
parameterisation *is* the change — 27 `main()` locals became keyword-only
arguments — so there is no "strip the prefixes and diff". #1380's review made the
point and asked that it be stated rather than left for a verifier to assume the
same guarantee is available.

### The mutation check, and what it found

Keyword-only arguments stop the call site's *order* drifting. They do nothing
about a **transposition** between two same-shaped arguments, and 18 of the inputs
sit in such a group. The review asked for a deliberate swap-and-confirm-it-fails
check.

**13 transpositions probed, 13 caught, 0 undiscriminated** — and by two different
mechanisms, which is better than the review assumed:

| group | pairs | how a swap is caught |
|---|---:|---|
| load, solar arrays | 6 | **impossible, not merely detected** — `solver/elements.py:1231`'s band validation (`lower <= forecast <= upper`) raises `ValueError` at construction |
| price, kW, scalars | 7 | a genuinely different plan: different dispatch, different total cost |

So a third of the inputs the review worried about cannot produce a wrong plan at
all. Asserted separately, so if either mechanism weakens the failure says which.
Every swap is preceded by an assertion that the two fixture values actually
**differ**, and the fixture sets both price-risk-aversion dials non-zero on
purpose: at 0.0 an `import_price`/`import_price_upper` swap is a true no-op and
the check would pass vacuously. **Probing before writing the assertions was the
right order** — writing them first and discovering they pass vacuously is the
failure mode this avoids.

## Two measurement findings filed rather than left in a comment

**#1416 — the dispatch report's "$X of it in the top 4 hours" uses a different
denominator than the total it is "of".** `regret` is the full `total_cost`
difference including the one-time `salvage_value` term; the hourly buckets
deliberately exclude it. So the hourly figures can sum to **more** than the day
total they are presented as a share of — measured $7.36 "of" a $7.04 total on the
2026-09-27 report. `hourly_regret_breakdown()`'s own docstring is scrupulous about
the gap, and `quality_report.py` acknowledges it twice; **the report is the only
place that presents the two as parts of one whole, and the only place with no
caveat.** Filed because #1318 is closed and the finding would otherwise live in a
comment on a closed issue.

**#1417 — #1406 changed the proximal penalty's SHAPE but not its WEIGHT, and the
weight was calibrated for the old shape.** This one is against myself: #1406's own
text said *"re-derive `w` downward once uniform"*, 116KAT's fix-(a) said the same,
and I shipped the structural change at the unchanged live 0.045.

Measured on production's real tier structure — 201 periods, 2 at 1 min, 9 at 5 min,
190 at 30 min — for a 40 kW deviation:

| scope | before (`w × hours`) | after (`w`) | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `period[0]` — the chatter case | $0.030 | $1.80 | **60×** |
| first hour | $1.41 | $19.80 | **14×** |
| whole horizon | $172.41 | $361.80 | 2.1× |

The 60× is the intent and it works: a degenerate flip at the measured p90 spread
earns $0.027 against a $1.80 anchor. **The 14× on the first hour is the part nobody
asked for** — the break-even spread a genuine near-term re-plan must beat rose from
0.045 to **0.632 $/kWh**.

I deliberately did **not** conclude that is a regression. The tempting comparison is
116KAT's measured 0.117–0.122 $/kWh "moves worth keeping" band, but that is
*adjacent-period spread within one plan* — the smoothness domain — while this is
*cross-solve* deviation. Reaching for the nearest number because the units match is
exactly the category error that got #1406 filed wrong in the first place. The right
quantity is unmeasured, and the issue says what would measure it.

Derived candidate, with its caveat: `w = w_old × H/N = 0.0214` holds whole-horizon
behaviour identical to pre-#1406 while still giving `period[0]` a $0.86 anchor, 31×
the degenerate-flip gain. Not optimal — `H/N` depends on the tier structure, which
#451 made adaptive — but it is the minimum I should have done. The weight is a live
dashboard entity, so any install seeing over-stickiness can fix it in seconds.

### And the other half of #1406 verified, since I had asserted it three times

The claim that smoothness is right to **keep** `hours[t]` because `h` cancels: it
holds, and exactly — `penalty = w × h × Δ` against `gain = spread × Δ × h` gives a
break-even spread of **exactly `w`, identical in every tier**.

One precision I had overstated: "uniform across tiers" is exact *within* a tier and
wrong *at* a boundary, because the penalty is priced by `hours[t]` — the **later**
period — while the energy that can move is bounded by `hours[t-1]`. Break-even rises
to 5× at the 1min→5min boundary and 6× at 5min→30min. **Scope: exactly 2 of ~200
transitions**, both inside the first hour. Recorded on #1417 rather than filed, since
a fix would have to choose between `hours[t]`, `hours[t-1]` and `min(...)` with no
measurement saying which.

The contrast is the useful part: smoothness has a genuine cancellation, so leaving it
alone needed no re-derivation. Proximal has no cancelling term, which is why changing
its shape without touching its weight moved a real threshold by 14×.

## #1434 — the gate's blind spot, measured instead of estimated (PR #1436)

`noop_patches.py` watches `patch.object` and `monkeypatch.setattr`. It cannot see
`module.attr = value`. For `_NATIVE_HASS` it observes **9 of 201** sites.

#1436 adds a static report (`--report-untracked`): **317 direct-assignment sites across 45
test files**. #1401's figure was **231** because it counted only the two tracked forms.

**One heuristic detail worth keeping, because the first version was wrong.** Matching
`<name>.<attr> = ...` on the name alone produced six false positives on `coordinator.data`
— `coordinator` is a fixture, not a module. The fix requires the base name to be *imported
as a module in that same file*, which also picks up aliased imports the naive version
missed (`fss._store_for`). Precision and recall both improved from the same constraint.

The **runtime** half of #1434 stays open. Closing it needs a `ModuleType.__setattr__`
override, which is a different and more invasive mechanism than a static scan.

## #1405 — reaping stale worktrees at startup (PR #1440, merged)

**The teardown was already correct, and #1354 had already hardened it.** That is what
reframed this issue: the fix does not belong in teardown.

Measured on the reference machine: **28** leaked `coverage-compare-*` trees, ~614 MB, of
which **22 from a single day** — bursty, not a drip. They split cleanly:

| contents | count | means |
|---|---:|---|
| no `wt-*` left | **11** | worktree removal **succeeded**; the temp dir survived anyway |
| `wt-head`/`wt-base` present | 16 | removal never ran |

And nothing was locked — **0 read-only files, `shutil.rmtree` succeeds on retry**. So
`TemporaryDirectory.__exit__` did not *fail*; it **never ran**. No `finally`, no
`__exit__`, no `atexit` survives a `SIGKILL`, and these gates spawn pytest children that
run for tens of minutes. **Only startup reaping can close it**, with an age threshold
(default 6 h) so a concurrent run is never pulled out from under itself.

### The test that mutated the real machine

The first version read `tempfile.gettempdir()` unconditionally, so **its own test suite
deleted 27 real leaked trees (~613 MB) and deregistered 16 real worktrees** on this box.
Benign, and exactly what the issue recommended — but **not a side effect anyone asked a
test to have**, it could have raced a live gate run, and I had twice said I would not clear
the household's disk unprompted.

Fixed with an injectable `tmp_root`. **Hermeticity proven rather than asserted**: a decoy
tree planted in the real temp dir, the suite run, the decoy still there afterwards. Suite
time also fell **5.57s → 0.91s**, because it stopped walking the real temp directory.
