## #919 shipped to `main` (PR #936), and the sensor choice was the whole fix

A deployed install can now answer *"is the ML load forecaster actually beating
naive persistence on my data?"* — which no HACS install, on any household, had
ever been able to answer. `forecast_regret.py` has computed it since #273, but
only ever driven by `reference_benchmark.py` against synthetic scenarios.

**The design published on the issue the night before was wrong, and wrong in the
direction that would have looked like success.** It proposed recovering the
forecast trail from `sensor.nimbus_household_load_total_forecast`'s recorded
state, on the correct observation that its state is `round(load_kw[0], 3)`.

But `solver_inputs/load.py` overwrites `load_kw[0]` immediately before that
publish — #429's live anchor, which is correct and doing its job:

```python
live_load_kw = float(sw.ha_get(whole_house_cross_check_sensor)["state"])
load_kw[0] = max(0.0, live_load_kw)
```

`whole_house_cross_check_sensor` is **the same sensor
`_compute_report_for_window()` uses as its real-load ground truth**. So the
proposed trail is the ground truth echoed back. It would have driven
`j_forecast` onto `j_star` and published near-perfect forecast skill on every
install, forever.

And not as an edge case: a report returns `None` without that sensor
configured, and the anchor fires exactly when it is configured. **The
precondition for computing the metric was the precondition for it being fake** —
100% of installs that could publish it.

Confirmed live before concluding it, three hours of real recorded history:

| time | proposed "forecast" | real load |
|---|---|---|
| 01:40:29 | 0.90 | 0.90 |
| 01:39:25 | 0.91 | 0.91 |
| 01:38:54 | 0.97 | 0.97 |
| 01:37:32 | 0.94 | 0.94 |

Identical to the cent on every row, a few seconds apart.

**The fix is a different sensor.** `whole_house_now_kw` and `summed_18_now_kw`
are both snapshotted *before* the anchor, precisely so #429's cross-check
compares two genuine forecasts, and both are already flattened to recorded
entities. The right one is
`sensor.nimbus_solver_load_whole_house_cross_check_now_kw` — the forecast **of**
the ground-truth sensor, so forecast-of-X against measured-X with nothing to
reconcile. Over the same window it read 1.001–1.052 against a real 0.89–0.97:
real error, ~10% high.

Two deliberate scope decisions, both recorded in the module docstring:

- **Solar is held at its real measured values in every scenario.** Nimbus
  publishes no solar nowcast — Solcast/Open-Meteo do, and their accuracy is not
  this project's to report. Holding solar at truth makes the delta attributable
  to *load* forecast quality alone. The perfect-trail test is what proves this is
  actually wired that way: if solar were not held at truth, a perfect load trail
  would still carry solar-attributable regret and that test would fail.
- **Named `load_nowcast_skill_*` throughout.** The trail measures one-step-ahead
  skill, not the day-ahead forecast the dispatch was built on. Both are real
  questions and they are different ones; the prefix stops anyone comparing it
  against `reference_benchmark.py`'s own day-ahead figure. The naming call was
  taken rather than holding the build another day for it.

A negative `value_add_dollars` publishes plainly rather than being suppressed —
an install where persistence wins deserves to be told.

21 new tests. Full local suite on a clean, unswitched tree: 2685 passed, 14
skipped, 334 subtests, exit 0.

## #933: a published negative result was wrong

The day before, the near-zero summed-forecast condition was reported as "not
observed", resting on 24 h of raw history and on a belief that only two days of
long-term statistics existed. **Eleven days exist, and the summed 18-circuit
total hit exactly 0.0 on five of them** (08-27 → 08-30 consecutive, plus 09-01,
whose daily *mean* is also only 0.359 kW against 1.0–2.5 kW either side).

Exactly 0.0 is the tell. HA's statistics compiler skips non-numeric states, so
`unavailable` is excluded rather than stored as zero — a recorded minimum of 0.0
means the sensor genuinely published `0.0`, which on the summed path requires
*every* contributing circuit to have taken the `continue` branch. An 18-circuit
household cannot draw exactly nothing.

Raw history for those dates is purged, so the cause is undetermined, and the
**benign reading is the more likely one**: circuits still being added and trained
in late August fail the fetch exactly like circuits that are transiently down,
and 09-01's depressed mean fits "many circuits not reporting yet" rather than a
brief glitch. But the cause changes the expected *frequency*, not the
*reachability* — and "not observed" was the only thing holding the fix back.

Recommended a refinement safer than either variant the issue proposed: gate on
`failed_entities and nonzero_fraction < 0.1` rather than the near-zero test
alone. Otherwise a household adding circuits one at a time would see Nimbus
refuse to plan with no visible reason. Strictly narrower than 10%-alone, strictly
wider than all-failed-alone, and it still catches the 14-of-18 partial case.
Awaiting the household's decision; it changes solve behaviour, so it holds
overnight under #594 regardless.

## #945 — a WARNING stream that buried its own signal (PR #947)

99 WARNINGs in 63 minutes, every one `consecutive skips: 1`. #315 made every

### The version collision, and why neither number was usable

PRs #930 and #931 each carried a `manifest.json` bump *inside a feature branch*,
to v0.94.331 and v0.94.332. They collided the moment #930 merged. Resolving that
collision in favour of either number made the #594 guard fail:

```
AssertionError: Lists differ: ['0.94.331'] != []
```

The guard enforces `_MIN_ENFORCED_VERSION <= v < in_flight`. **Neither version
was ever tagged** -- `git tag -l 'v0.94.33*'` returned only `v0.94.330` -- so
`main` was green solely because `.331` was itself the in-flight version and thus
outside its own window. Bumping past it pushed `.331` inside, where it had no
`Devhub validation:` line it could honestly carry: nothing had been deployed at
v0.94.331, because no such release existed.

The fix was not to back-write a validation claim. It was to take the bump out of
the feature branch entirely -- which is what this repo's own documented
convention already says -- fold the never-tagged section into the real release,
and use `.332` rather than reusing `.331`, because `main` had already carried
`0.94.331` in `manifest.json` with different code than a `v0.94.331` tag would
now hold. One version string, two code states, on a project whose reference
deploy is a raw git clone.

**A near-miss worth recording:** the release PR body initially listed #951 as
shipping. It had not merged -- #958 was still open, fully green, and simply
never merged. Caught by a direct question rather than by any check. Merged
first, then folded into the release so the body matched what the tag actually
contained.

### All four IV&V findings closed

Mark Purcell's #950 pass produced four issues; all four shipped in v0.94.333.

**#953** -- `discharge_vars` built its bounds from two conditions that never
consulted each other: `ub` honoured `gated_periods`, `lb` did not. Both a
positive `spike_override_discharge_kw` and period 0 masked away produced a

The part worth re-reading is what the tests corrected. A test asserting #779's

gates nothing, and on an unavailable one `spike_active_now` requires

source that can gate period 0 while the override is live -- exactly the scope
Mark identified, but arrived at by a test failing rather than by agreeing with
him in advance.

**#952** -- the #357 anti-drift guard listed `solver_inputs/*.py` alone, so
`solver_publish.py` was invisible to it. Registering it immediately surfaced

**#954** -- the #495/#452 discovery rules were covered only by hand-copied

**#955** -- the source-comment guard checks a referenced `test_*.py` exists,
never that it contains the claimed guard. Two content heuristics were measured
against the real corpus rather than reasoned about: a bidirectional issue link
passes 6 of the 11 references carrying an issue number; an enclosing-symbol match
passes 7 of 12, with 10 of 22 having no enclosing symbol at all. Both would fail
on ~40% of a corpus containing no real defects, and a guard that cries wolf gets
an allowlist bolted on and then ignored. So the ceiling is stated plainly, and
**coverage** was extended instead of depth -- the sweep now reads the
standalone/cron writer, real shipped source with 5 references and no guard on
any of them.

Each fix shipped with a durable half, because re-extracting or re-registering one

had noticed. A guard that cannot fail is indistinguishable from a guard that
works, which is the #757 lesson restated in a different register.

## Late morning: the EPR guard, and a hypothesis killed by its own instrument

**v0.94.334** ships #956's guard: a negative `regret_dollars` no longer publishes as
though it were a score. `epr_reliable` goes False, a once-per-scored-day WARNING names
the cause, and the report carries the evidence (`epr_reason`,
`achieved_within_lp_soc_bounds`, `achieved_below_floor_kwh`, `lp_soc_envelope_pct`).

### The mechanism, verified — then immediately qualified

The decisive test had been *named* in that issue's own comments days earlier and never
run. Run against the reference household's published 2026-09-15 report: Min SoC
configured at **2.0%**, the oracle sitting precisely on 2.0% for two consecutive hours,
and the achieved trajectory finishing at **0.4429%** — **1.90 kWh the oracle is
structurally forbidden from selling.** Priced against a strictly larger feasible set,
the achieved dispatch can beat "optimal".

It also killed the fix the issue originally proposed. #571's `out_of_range` read False
on all 24 hours of that day, because it tests `[0, 100]` — physical possibility — not
the LP's `[min_pct, max_pct]`. 0.4429 is inside `[0, 100]`. **Clamping to `[0, 100]`
would not have changed that day at all.** And it reframes #949: not a second symptom of
one drift, but a diagnostic checking the wrong bound and therefore blind to exactly the
condition causing the other.

**Then the guard corrected its own author, within minutes of deploying.** The first live
WARNING came back on the `oracle_beaten` branch — the one written for "this mechanism
does not explain this one":

> the achieved SoC trajectory stayed INSIDE the LP's own envelope, so the mechanism
> verified on the reference household does not explain this one

Achieved minimum 8.93% against a 2.0% floor. No breach. Regret still negative. The PR
and changelog had said "confirmed"; that was too strong. The sub-floor excursion is a
real route, not a necessary condition.

### Two hypotheses, one dead and one strong

**Terminal-value credit — dead, by reading the code.** `regret.py`'s docstring describes
a 2026-08-29 fix for precisely the EPR > 100% symptom and calls it opt-in (*"Both None
is a complete no-op"*), which reads exactly like an unwired guard of the #538/#692 kind.
It is not: `quality_report.py` strips `salvage_value`/`headroom_value`/
`terminal_value_breakpoints` from **all three** trajectories. `solver_salvage_value` is
0.15 on this install and the scorer never sees it.

**The P2P pin — strong, and it resolves a question left explicitly open.** The oracle's
flat −12.0 kW across 17:00–23:00 was hedged in an earlier comment as "at least as
consistent with a pin as with a cap". Config settles it: `solver_grid_max_export_kw` is
**40**, `solver_p2p_block_1_rate_kw` is **12** over hours 17–24. It is the pin.

That matters more than a cap would have, because **a pin is an equality**. The oracle is
forced to export exactly 12 kW for seven hours; the realized evaluation applies no such
constraint and prices whatever happened — 12.39 to 12.77 kW. At this install's own
realised P2P rate (~$0.218/kWh, from `real_p2p_dollars`/`real_p2p_volume_kwh`) that is
~$0.56 of advantage against a −$0.7307 regret, **with no SoC bound involved at all**.

One fact deliberately not smoothed over: a fresh service-call rescoring of the same day
shows the oracle exporting 19.37 kW at 17:00, so the pin was not applied in that path.
Why the same window pins in the scheduled run and not the service call is unestablished.

### What it means for the fix

Clamping the achieved integration now looks wrong rather than merely weak — neither
verified mechanism is addressed by it. If the pin asymmetry is the real story, the honest
fix is on the **oracle's** side: score against an oracle subject to the same commitments
the day actually operated under, or exclude pinned periods. That is #768's territory,
not a patch here. #956 stays open for that and for the clamping decision.

### The day's shape, restated

Morning: three guards found enforcing less than they appeared to, two of them with
nothing failing. Late morning: a fourth — and then the newly-built instrument
immediately overturning its own author's stated conclusion. The pattern holds in both
directions now: the thing that catches a wrong claim is always an instrument pointed at
the site that produces the value, and the useful ones are the ones built to be able to
say *"this does not explain it."*
