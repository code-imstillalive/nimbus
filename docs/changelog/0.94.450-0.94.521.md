## [0.94.519] - 2026-11-05

**The Regret tab no longer loads a small install while you edit your dashboard.**

- **One quality report per day, one at a time** (#2276). The Regret tab's three cards each ask for their day's score whenever the tab renders: on a page load, and on every save in the dashboard editor. Each request was a full hindsight solve, about 5 s on the test box, and three at once took 15 s. On a tester's smaller box, deleting and renaming tabs stalled Home Assistant for about five minutes. Now `nimbus_load.compute_quality_report` runs one report at a time, and a day scored in the last 10 minutes is answered from memory, so the three cards and every re-render share one solve. *Re-score* (`nimbus_load.rescore_history`) clears what is remembered. Scores are unchanged.
- The dashboards guide says how to add the Nimbus Tabs card when your tabs were pasted before 0.94.507 and have no *Check for updates* button (#2275). The setup guide's numbers table lists the Site import limit (#2273).

## [0.94.516] - 2026-11-04

**A live site import limit: the battery never pushes your property over its supply.** Safety feature.

- **Site Import Limit** (#2257, #2258, reviewed by Mark Purcell). The plan keeps the battery and the *forecast* house load under Grid Max Import, but an EV, an oven or a kettle can switch on at any moment. One household's 46 kW supply tripped with a 30 kW grid charge, a 10.7 kW car, hot water and pool, and a toaster. Set `number.nimbus_solver_site_import_limit_kw` (Solver tab) to your supply's trip point less room for an appliance switching on, for example 44 kW against 46. **0 = off, the default**, so nothing changes until you set it.
  - `sensor.nimbus_battery_allowed_charge_kw`: the most the battery may charge right now without grid import passing the limit, from the live grid meter. It counts a battery that is discharging now, since that supply stops when it switches to charge (Mark's review). It is unavailable while the limit is off or a reading is missing.
  - `switch.nimbus_solver_grid_meter_positive_is_import` (default on): your grid meter's sign. The sensor's `grid_kw` attribute shows the reading it used, so you can check it.
  - **The dispatch blueprint** never sends a charge above the allowance (new input *Nimbus allowed charge*, defaulting to the sensor), and answers a load switching on within seconds.
  - **New blueprint *Nimbus site import guard*** trims any charge, manual or another automation's, once import passes the limit. It never raises it.
  - **The going-live guide** has a new section, *Your site's supply limit*, and a new row in the failsafe table. Re-import the dispatch blueprint to get the cap.
- First slice: import only. Export follows once this is proven (Mark, #2257). The reference household has run the same guard as an automation since 4 Nov 08:45: through a car charge beside 35–38 kW battery charging, the import peaked at 44.7 kW against its 46 kW supply, with no trip.

### Added
- **Does stochastic earn more? A closed-loop paired replay** (#1797
  programme, PR C): `scripts/stochastic_replay.py`, read-only, on what
  `nimbus_load.export_decision_inputs` returns. Every full-horizon record is
  one decision; each arm plans from it at the SoC its own replay has
  reached, follows its plan until the next decision, and is settled against
  what happened (each record's live row 0: solar, load and the price) at the
  same prices, with the end SoC valued at the salvage value for every arm.

  falling back where drive would) and `p50` (no solar risk aversion), so the
  hedge is not credited with what dropping the 25% solar derate alone would
  do. Per-day differences and a moving-block bootstrap 95% interval. The

  default, with a switch to drive** (#1797). The full model `build_plan()`
  builds (every battery, load, controllable and thermal load, P2P block,

  scenario, p10 / p50 / p90 from the solar band, weighted 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.3,
  all in one joint problem. Every decision in the first 30 minutes (battery,

  published is the p50 scenario's, so period 0 is the shared decision.

  non-anticipativity gap read back off the solution) and

  runs in the background at most once every 5 minutes (hourly after one
  slower than 20 s). `switch.nimbus_solver_stochastic_dispatch` on makes the

- **Two-stage stochastic dispatch: drive mode made safe to switch on, and
  the shadow solve taken off the dispatch path** (#1797 programme, PR A).
  Found by a line-by-line audit of #1805 and a probe that drove
  `run_stochastic_dispatch()` through each path:
  - **Shadow prices.** In a joint solve, p50's own balance-row dual carries
    the other scenarios' marginals through non-anticipativity: period 0 read
    2.5x the expected marginal. Every dual and reduced cost of the joint solve
    is now the expected marginal (`w_p50 * sum of the scenario copies`), so
    `energy_shadow_price_now`, the adequacy marginal cost, the guard's
    shadow-price series, flex telemetry and the forced-import reduced costs
    read a driven plan in the same units as a deterministic one. With a band
    too narrow to change any decision they match the deterministic plan's.
  - **The shadow solve no longer delays dispatch.** It ran before publish,
    holding the dispatched plan back 3-14 s every 5 minutes and inflating
    `solve_seconds`. It now runs on its own background thread, on copies of
    the inputs; each cycle publishes the latest finished summary.
  - **Drive backs off.** A joint timeout was never "slow", so a MIP install
    retried (~20 s) every cycle; an exception warned every cycle. Every
    fallback now waits 5 min, doubling to an hour, and each reason is logged
    once. The drive solve has a total budget of 10 s across every joint call
    `build_plan()` makes (thermal retry, direction rounds), not 20 s each.
  - **Drive never dispatches a weaker plan.** It falls back when the joint
    plan relaxed a thermal deadline or left flows two-way where the
    deterministic plan did not, when non-anticipativity is not honoured, when
    a scenario cost is not finite, and when the solar band is the envelope
    rather than a calibrated p10/p90 (#1807) -- whose hedge is nominal.
    A joint solver error no longer triggers network.py's thermal
    relax-and-retry, and network.py's own retry log lines are DEBUG on the
    joint path.
  - **Signals from one source.** A driven plan takes the offer curve and
    every ranging signal (grid, battery, load) from the deterministic solve;
    battery and load signals used to be empty.
  - **Switching drive off** re-published the drive summary (`drives_dispatch`
    true) for up to 5 minutes. A mode change now resets the cadence.
  - **solver/lp.py:** the phased-solve cooldown no longer turns the joint
    `BlendedOptions` solve into a primary-only one still reported as
    "blended"; the #1360 Unknown-retry counters published in
    `solve_diagnostics` count the live solve only.
  - **Summary:** gains each scenario's terminal SoC, `thermal_guarantee_relaxed`
    and `direction_exclusivity`; `raw_status` is the solve behind the plan, not
    a later failed round. The module docstring no longer claims a load risk

    forecast risk dial does nothing.
